Question 1 of 10

What is the simple interest on ₹1000 at 5% per annum for 2 years?

SI = (P × R × T)/100 = (1000 × 5 × 2)/100 = ₹100.
Question 2 of 10

Find the simple interest on ₹2000 at 10% per annum for 3 years.

SI = (2000 × 10 × 3)/100 = ₹600.
Question 3 of 10

What principal amount gives ₹300 as simple interest at 6% per annum for 5 years?

SI = (P×R×T)/100 → 300 = (P×6×5)/100 → P = 30000/30 = ₹1000.
Question 4 of 10

What is the compound interest on ₹1000 at 10% per annum for 2 years (compounded annually)?

Amount = 1000×(1.10)² = 1210; CI = 1210 - 1000 = ₹210.
Question 5 of 10

At what rate percent per annum will ₹500 amount to ₹600 in 4 years at simple interest?

SI = 100; Rate = (SI×100)/(P×T) = (100×100)/(500×4) = 5%.
Question 6 of 10

Find the simple interest on ₹5000 at 8% per annum for 6 months.

6 months = 0.5 years; SI = (5000×8×0.5)/100 = ₹200.
Question 7 of 10

The compound interest is always ___ the simple interest for the same principal, rate, and time (beyond year 1).

Because compound interest earns interest on interest, it exceeds simple interest after the first period.
Question 8 of 10

What is the amount after 1 year on a principal of ₹2000 at 10% simple interest?

SI = (2000×10×1)/100 = 200; Amount = 2000+200 = ₹2200.
Question 9 of 10

A sum doubles itself in 8 years at simple interest. What is the rate of interest?

If sum doubles, SI = P over 8 years: Rate = (100×P)/(P×8) = 12.5%.
Question 10 of 10

Find the compound interest on ₹1500 at 20% per annum for 1 year, compounded annually.

For 1 year, compound interest equals simple interest: CI = 1500 × 20% = ₹300.
